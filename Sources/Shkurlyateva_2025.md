## Summary

**The following is a paraphrase of the publisher's English abstract only. The article body has not been read; nothing below should be treated as a claim about what the article argues on any specific page.**

The abstract frames the article's subject as the collective religious identity of Lutherans as "liturgical Protestants" with a rich musical culture. Its conceptual starting point is Anatoly Pogasy's concept of competing religious discourses: on this model, two rival discourses emerged in the sixteenth century — a Lutheran discourse and a Reformed (Zwinglian, Calvinist) discourse — and their dialectical interaction continues today. Hymnals are treated as part of a religious community's cultural code, carrying dense semiosis; the stated method is a comparative analysis of Lutheran hymnals set against the hymnals of other Protestant communities. The abstract singles out the controversy between Martin Luther and Andreas Karlstadt over the reform of worship as a point of particular attention.

The abstract states the article's conclusion in two parts. First, the concept of **adiaphora** is presented as the key to Lutheran musical and liturgical discourse: on this reading, Lutherans made a free, conscious choice to preserve the historic liturgical system — worship practice, the system of church books, the liturgical calendar — rather than being bound to it by doctrinal necessity. Second, the abstract lists the main markers of Lutheran musical-liturgical identity that the article identifies: the very existence of a hymnal, its structure, Gregorian chant, instrumental accompaniment, and "dogmatic depth." The article also states that it formulates a definition of a Lutheran hymn in both a broad and a narrow sense, though the abstract does not give the content of that distinction.

1. Lutheran collective religious identity is constituted, in part, through liturgical practice and a distinctively rich musical culture — Lutherans as "liturgical Protestants." (per abstract)
2. The analysis is built on Anatoly Pogasy's concept of competing religious discourses: a Lutheran discourse and a Reformed (Zwinglian/Calvinist) discourse emerged as rivals in the sixteenth century, and their dialectical interaction continues into the present. (per abstract)
3. Hymnals function as part of a religious community's cultural code and carry a dense semiosis — they are not incidental to confessional identity but constitutive of it. (per abstract)
4. Method: comparative analysis of Lutheran hymnals against the hymnals of other Protestant communities. (per abstract)
5. The Luther–Karlstadt controversy over the reform of worship receives particular attention as a formative episode behind the Lutheran liturgical-musical settlement. (per abstract)
6. Central conclusion: **adiaphora** is the key concept structuring Lutheran musical-liturgical discourse.
