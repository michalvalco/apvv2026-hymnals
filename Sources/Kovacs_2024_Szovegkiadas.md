## What it contains — provisional

Per the publisher's description and the OSZK launch announcement: a **study together with a text edition**, presenting the biblical-Czech printed hymns of the old Kingdom of Hungary from the beginnings to 1701, **with Hungarian translations**, framed as helping the reader understand the cultural, religious and historical significance of these melodies and texts.

Per `Kovacs_2025_BiblikusCseh` n. 2 — the author's own description, and the most reliable statement available — this is **a critical edition and translation of *selected* texts** from the verses catalogued in the 2022 repertory.

⚠️ **Do not attribute the figure 2,335 to this volume.** That is the 2022 repertory's count of catalogued incipits. A secondary summary of the launch event repeated it here; the author's own footnote says *selected* texts, and 347 pages could not carry 2,335 edited and translated poems. **The selection principle and the number of texts edited are unknown and must be checked against the book.**

**Launched 8 May 2025** at the National Széchényi Library (Alapítók tere, sixth floor), in a joint OSZK / Szent Adalbert Foundation event, alongside Mészáros Andor's *A csehszlovakizmus története az első világháborúig*. Presented by **Mészáros Andor** (ELTE, Institute of History) and **Kiss Gy. Csaba** (literary historian, Széchenyi Prize, DSc).

## Why this is the more useful of the two volumes for this chapter

The 2022 repertory tells you **that** a poem exists and where it was printed. This one gives you **the text**.

For a chapter arguing transfer and adaptation — that Czech material was received and re-framed in Slovak Lutheran use — an incipit index establishes the population, but the argument has to be made on **texts set side by side**. That is what a critical edition is for. And the Hungarian translations, while not the chapter's working language, are a **control on reading**: where the biblical Czech is difficult, a scholarly translation by the cataloguer of the whole corpus is a check that no dictionary provides.

⚠️ **The critical apparatus is the real prize and the real unknown.** A critical edition of texts with Czech antecedents should record variants — and variants between a Bohemian original and its Hungarian-Kingdom reprint are *precisely* the "Lutheranisation" the chapter argues for, in their most direct evidentiary form. `Kovacs_2025_BiblikusCseh` establishes that such editing was the norm (RMNy crediting a Levoča editor as co-author of a 1683 reissue). **Whether this volume's apparatus exposes those variants is the single most important thing to establish on getting it, and nothing available now answers it.**
